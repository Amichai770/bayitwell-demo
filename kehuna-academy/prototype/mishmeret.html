Why two moments

In the Mikdash the whole day was held between two lambs. Nothing was offered before the morning Tamid, and nothing after the Tamid of the afternoon. The Rambam brings this in Hilchot Temidin uMusafin, chapter one. We have no lamb. The frame is still ours.

Sixty seconds of kavana

The verse after the blessing says: וַאֲנִי אֲבָרְכֵם, "and I will bless them." The blessing is not yours. You are the open hand it passes through. The Zohar in Naso teaches that the Shechinah rests upon the hands of the Kohanim while they bless. So before the first word, empty the hands. Nothing to prove, nothing to give from your own store. Then say the Name, and let the blessing go where it is sent.

Mishmar מִשְׁמָר
The 24 watches

Every Kohen belongs to a watch

In the Mikdash the Kohanim served in twenty-four mishmarot, listed in Divrei HaYamim I, chapter 24. Each watch came up to Jerusalem for one week, twice a year. Here it works the same way. Every learner joins a mishmar. When your watch is on duty, your circle hosts the week's discussion and receives the live class.

Day one

You and your sons: what is kept

וְאַתָּה וּבָנֶיךָ אִתְּךָ תִּשְׁמְרוּ אֶת־כְּהֻנַּתְכֶם
"And you and your sons with you shall keep your priesthood."
Bamidbar 18:7
Teaching

The verse does not say you inherited your priesthood. It says you shall keep it. Tishmeru. The same root as mishmeret, a watch. A Kohen receives the Kehuna by birth, and there is nothing to do about that. A kept Kehuna is something else. It is a priesthood that someone is actually watching over, the way a guard watches a gate at night.

So the question this whole path is asking is simple. What does it look like to keep a priesthood in a year with no Mikdash, no korbanot, and a day that looks like everyone else's? We are going to answer it one piece at a time. Today, only notice this: you were given something to keep.

Why does the Kohen not own land?
Because G-d Himself is the Kohen's portion. When the land is divided, Aharon is told: אֲנִי חֶלְקְךָ וְנַחֲלָתְךָ, "I am your portion and your inheritance among the children of Israel." The Rambam explains that the tribe of Levi was set apart to serve G-d and to teach His ways, and so it was freed from the ordinary wars and labors of the land. Then he adds a remarkable line: not only Levi, but any person in the world whose spirit moves him to set himself apart to stand before G-d and serve Him becomes holy of holies, and G-d becomes his portion. In Rabbi Amichai's conversation with Eli, this is why people could trust the Kohen with their impurity. A man who owns nothing is not a threat to anyone. He can stand between. Bamidbar 18:20 · Devarim 18:1–2 · Rambam, Shemitah veYovel 13:12–13 · Live Kabbalah, "The Kehuna: Priesthood, Hod and the work of Shalom" (May 2025)
