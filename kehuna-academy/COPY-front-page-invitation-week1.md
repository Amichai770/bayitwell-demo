## 1. Front page (kehunacademy.com, under the new name)

**Hero**

Mishmeret
מִשְׁמֶרֶת

For the Kohanim of today.

You were given something to keep. Mishmeret is a daily watch of the priesthood: two minutes in the morning and two at sunset, a Beit Midrash for the laws and the inner meaning of the Kehuna, and a mishmar of Kohanim around the world who keep it with you.

[Join the first mishmar] (free)
[Learn the Rambam's Laws of the Temple]

**The verse**

וְאַתָּה וּבָנֶיךָ אִתְּךָ תִּשְׁמְרוּ אֶת־כְּהֻנַּתְכֶם
"And you and your sons with you shall keep your priesthood." Bamidbar 18:7

The Torah does not say you inherited your priesthood. It says you shall keep it. A Kohen receives the Kehuna by birth. A kept Kehuna is something else. It is a priesthood someone is actually watching over, the way a guard watches a gate at night. Mishmeret is that watch, for a year with no Mikdash, no korbanot, and a day that looks like everyone else's.

**What a day looks like**

Tamid. Morning and bein ha'arbayim, the two moments that held the whole day in the Mikdash. One source, one short teaching, one question, one thing placed on the altar. Two minutes.

Duchan. Birkat Kohanim word by word. The plain meaning, the Rashi, and a kavana to bring up with you for each of the fifteen words.

Mishmar. The Kohanim served in twenty-four watches. Every learner here joins one. When your watch is on duty, your circle hosts the week's question and receives the live class.

Beit Midrash. Thirty days on the Kohen today. The Rambam's Hilchot Beit HaBechira, chapter by chapter. The inner Kehuna in Chassidus and Kabbalah.

Ask. A study companion that knows the sources of this Beit Midrash, cites them, and sends personal halachic questions to your rav.

**Who this is for**

Kohanim. Whether you duchen every morning or found out at a bar mitzvah that you are one, whether you learn daily or have not opened a sefer in years. The priesthood is yours either way. This is a place to keep it.

If you are not a Kohen and you love the Mikdash, you are welcome to learn here too. The Beit Midrash is open. The mishmarot are for Kohanim.

**From Rabbi Amichai**

I am a Kohen. Our family name is Cohen-Skali, from the priestly town of Dibdu in Morocco, and before that from Cordova in Spain. We are descendants of Tzadok, the Kohen Gadol of King David.

For years I taught the Rambam's Laws of the Temple under the name Kehuna Academy. What I learned is that a Kohen does not need more information first. He needs a daily way to keep what he was given. Mishmeret is that. It is small on purpose, two minutes twice a day, and it is meant to be kept for a long time.

May we see the Kohanim at their avodah, speedily in our days.

Rabbi Amichai Cohen

**A word on money**

Mishmeret is free. It is a passion project of Live Kabbalah, a registered 501(c)(3). If at some point you feel called to support it, there will be a place to do that. No one is asked, and nothing here is locked.

**Footer line**

Mishmeret was formerly Kehuna Academy. The Rambam classes are the same classes, in the same place.

## 3. Week one of the Tamid (WhatsApp, copy and paste)

Two messages a day. Morning before 9:00, afternoon about an hour before sunset. Each one is one source, one teaching, one question.

### Day 1, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

אֶת־הַכֶּבֶשׂ אֶחָד תַּעֲשֶׂה בַבֹּקֶר
"The one lamb you shall offer in the morning." Bamidbar 28:4

The day in the Mikdash did not begin with whatever came first. It began with the Tamid. Before any private offering, before any request, the Kohen gave something that was not about him. Tamid means constant. Not big, constant.

Before the phone, before the emails, what is the one thing you will offer first this morning?

### Day 1, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

תִּכּוֹן תְּפִלָּתִי קְטֹרֶת לְפָנֶיךָ מַשְׂאַת כַּפַּי מִנְחַת־עָרֶב
"Let my prayer be set before You as incense, the lifting of my hands as the evening offering." Tehillim 141:2

Bein ha'arbayim, between the evenings. The light has not gone, but it is going. This is the hour that is hardest to give, because the day has already spent us. David asks that the lifting of his hands be received like the evening offering. For a Kohen, lifted hands are not a metaphor.

What is still yours to give, now, at the end of the day?

### Day 2, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

וְאַתָּה וּבָנֶיךָ אִתְּךָ תִּשְׁמְרוּ אֶת־כְּהֻנַּתְכֶם
"And you and your sons with you shall keep your priesthood." Bamidbar 18:7

Not inherit. Keep. Tishmeru, the same root as mishmeret, a watch. You received the Kehuna at birth and there was nothing to do about it. A kept Kehuna is a different thing. It is one that someone is actually standing over.

Where in your ordinary week are you already standing watch, without calling it that?

### Day 2, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

שְׁמִירַת הַמִּקְדָּשׁ מִצְוַת עֲשֵׂה, וְאַף עַל פִּי שֶׁאֵין שָׁם פַּחַד מֵאוֹיְבִים
"Guarding the Mikdash is a positive mitzvah, even though there is no fear of enemies there." Rambam, Beit HaBechira 8:1

The Rambam says the guards were not there because of danger. They were there because a palace with guards is not the same as a palace without them. The guarding itself was the honor. Twenty-four posts, all night, every night.

What in your life is worth guarding tonight even though nothing is threatening it?

### Day 3, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

כֹּה תְבָרְכוּ אֶת־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל
"Thus you shall bless the children of Israel." Bamidbar 6:23

The Sifrei teaches that the Kohanim bless with a full heart. Not a formula recited, a blessing meant. And the verse after the blessing says, "and I will bless them." The blessing is G-d's. The Kohen is the open hand it passes through.

Whom will you bless today, with a full heart, without saying a word?

### Day 3, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

הֱוֵי מִתַּלְמִידָיו שֶׁל אַהֲרֹן, אוֹהֵב שָׁלוֹם וְרוֹדֵף שָׁלוֹם
"Be of the disciples of Aharon, loving peace and pursuing peace." Avot 1:12

Hillel did not say be a Kohen. He said be a student of the first one. Aharon's whole avodah outside the Mikdash was standing between two people who were not speaking. Not fixing it. Standing there.

Who are two people you could stand between this week?

### Day 4, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

וְהָיוּ עַל לֵב אַהֲרֹן בְּבֹאוֹ לִפְנֵי יְיָ
"And they shall be upon Aharon's heart when he comes before Hashem." Shemot 28:30

The names of the twelve tribes were set into the Choshen, and the Torah says where Aharon carried them. Not on his shoulders only. On his heart. A Kohen goes in carrying people. Before you go into your day, there are names you are carrying too, whether you chose them or not.

Whose name is on your heart this morning, and what would it mean to carry it in on purpose?

### Day 4, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

וְהָיָה אַהֲרֹן וּבָנָיו עֹשִׂים כֹּל
"And Aharon and his sons shall do everything." Bamidbar 4:19, on the work of the sons of Kehat

When the Kohathites carried the holiest vessels, they were not allowed to look at them uncovered. Aharon and his sons went in first and covered each one. The Kohen's work was often the work of covering, of protecting what is holy from being handled carelessly.

What did you cover today so that something holy would not be handled carelessly? What still needs covering before the day ends?

### Day 5, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

וְאֵשׁ הַמִּזְבֵּחַ תּוּקַד בּוֹ
"And the fire of the altar shall be kept burning on it." Vayikra 6:2

The fire on the Mizbeach was never allowed to go out, and it was the Kohen's job every morning to add wood before anything else. Not to light it. It was already burning. To keep it. Most of the Kehuna is like this. The fire is there. The work is the wood.

What is one piece of wood you can add this morning to a fire that is already burning in you?

### Day 5, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

בְּעֶרֶב אַתָּה מְקַטֵּר
"In the evening you shall burn the incense." From Shemot 30:8, on the Ketoret

Aharon burned the Ketoret twice a day, once in the morning when he tended the lamps and once at bein ha'arbayim when he lit them. The incense was the most inward part of the avodah, done alone, with no one watching. It left a scent and nothing else.

What is one thing you can do this evening that no one will see and that leaves only a scent?

### Day 6, morning

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

בְּהַעֲלֹתְךָ אֶת הַנֵּרֹת
"When you raise up the lamps." Bamidbar 8:2

Rashi asks why the Torah says to raise the lamps rather than to light them. He answers: the Kohen holds the flame to the wick until the flame rises on its own. That is what a Kohen does with a person too. He stays until the light stands by itself.

Who in your life needs you to hold the flame a little longer this morning, until it rises on its own?

### Day 6, afternoon

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

שָׁלוֹם שָׁלוֹם לָרָחוֹק וְלַקָּרוֹב
"Peace, peace, to the one who is far and to the one who is near." Yeshayahu 57:19

Erev Shabbat. The whole week of a Kohen ends where Birkat Kohanim ends, in shalom. The verse says it twice. The Sages read this as the one who is far first, and only then the one who is near. Peace begins with the person you have been avoiding.

Before Shabbat comes in, who is the far one, and what is one word of peace you can send?

### Day 7, Shabbat morning (for reading, not sending)

B"H
*Tamid shel Shachar*

וְעָרַךְ אֹתוֹ לִפְנֵי יְיָ תָּמִיד
"And he shall arrange it before Hashem always." Vayikra 24:8, on the Lechem HaPanim

Every Shabbat the Kohanim replaced the twelve loaves on the Shulchan, and the Torah says the old bread was still warm when it came off. A week is long, and the bread stayed fresh the whole time. The Kohen's work on Shabbat was to arrange, not to bake. Everything was already there.

What has stayed warm this whole week without your effort, and can you simply arrange it today?

### Day 7, Shabbat afternoon (for reading, not sending)

B"H
*Tamid shel Bein HaArbayim*

וַיִּשָּׂא אַהֲרֹן אֶת יָדָיו אֶל הָעָם וַיְבָרְכֵם
"And Aharon lifted his hands toward the people and blessed them." Vayikra 9:22

The first Birkat Kohanim in history happened on the eighth day of the Mishkan, at the end of the avodah, before the fire came down. Aharon did not wait to see whether it worked. He blessed first. That is the order. Bless, and then the fire.

As Shabbat goes out, whom will you bless before you know whether it worked?
